I like to track my own expenses so I know where every single dollar goes. For a long time that was a Google Sheet: fine, but slow for something I do several times a week & has little room for improvement. I know enough Google Sheets to be dangerous, and I also know how to code, so an application felt like the way to do more.  In addition, I've been wanting to get into self-hosting common services I use and this felt like a great way to get started.

## First attempt

In 2023, when I was just getting started with SwiftUI, I sketched the idea on a FigJam board to define a minimum viable product. By late 2025 the Mac app covered all of it: income and expenses, viewable in a calendar, a running list or a list by month. It worked, but it looked terrible and had no way to connect it to a backend.

## Second attempt

The rewrite, Penny-Pincher, starts from a contract instead of a screen. One self-hosted Go API with a SQLite database is described by an OpenAPI spec, and a React web app and a native Mac app are both built against it. Change the spec first, then the backend, then the clients.  My Staff Engineer on my team gave me the idea to try different frontier models for a month and see how work, thus I started with Claude so I could try out Claude Design & using Sonnet & Opis for a majority of the back and forth requirements gathering!

A few rules came out of the manual-entry habit:

- **No bank syncing.** Recording each transaction by hand is the point.
- **Money is integer cents.** Floats and money don't mix in the database so better to just stick with one format.
- **Derived, not stored.** The tax rate is `(gross taxable − net) ÷ gross taxable`, computed when it's shown, so it can never disagree with the numbers it came from.  Trying to keep the backend as simple as possible.

## What I'd tell my past self

Write down DETAILED requirements before the first screen, and keep the list of what the app will *not* do next to the list of what it will. Both made the second version smaller and better.
